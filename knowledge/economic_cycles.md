# Economic Cycles — Framework for Equity Investing

Understanding where the economy is in its cycle is not about market timing. It is about calibrating risk, choosing the right sectors, and avoiding the most common mistake: buying cyclical companies at peak earnings.

---

## 1. The Four Phases of the Economic Cycle

### Phase 1 — Early Recovery (Trough → Expansion)

**Macro signals:**
- GDP growth bottoming out and turning up
- Interest rates at/near lows; central bank signalling accommodation
- Credit growth beginning to pick up
- Corporate earnings troughing; analysts cutting estimates (then starting to revise up)
- Unemployment near cycle-highs but stabilising

**Best-performing sectors:** Financials (credit cycle turning), Autos, Consumer Discretionary, Industrials  
**Underperformers:** Utilities, Staples (already expensive from the flight to safety)

**Investor action:** Increase cyclical exposure; this phase offers the best risk-reward. Valuations are often compressed.

---

### Phase 2 — Mid-Cycle Expansion

**Macro signals:**
- GDP growing above trend
- Moderate inflation; central bank begins normalising rates
- Credit growth healthy; corporate borrowing for capex
- Earnings revisions broadly positive
- Consumer confidence high

**Best-performing sectors:** IT, FMCG, Healthcare (steady compounders shine), Specialty Chemicals  
**Underperformers:** Rate-sensitive sectors (banking under pressure from rising costs)

**Investor action:** Stay invested; focus on quality and earnings growth. Avoid paying excessive multiples for "sure things."

---

### Phase 3 — Late Cycle (Peak)

**Macro signals:**
- GDP at or above potential; labour market tight
- Inflation elevated; central bank aggressively tightening
- Credit growth peaking; spreads beginning to widen
- Commodity prices spiking
- Capex announcements at all-time highs (top signal)

**Best-performing sectors:** Energy, Commodities, Materials (hard assets), Utilities (late, but dividend appeal)  
**Underperformers:** High-growth, high-multiple stocks; small-caps; leveraged companies

**Investor action:** Reduce risk; trim cyclicals; shift to cash, defensives, and high-quality companies with pricing power. The party looks great; that is the risk.

---

### Phase 4 — Contraction / Recession

**Macro signals:**
- GDP contracting or near-zero
- Central bank cutting rates aggressively
- Credit tightening; NBFCs and weak banks under stress
- Earnings cuts, guidance withdrawals, management turnover
- Investor sentiment at extremes of fear

**Best-performing sectors:** Staples, Healthcare, Utilities, Gold  
**Underperformers:** Cyclicals, Financials (NPA cycle), Real Estate, Infrastructure

**Investor action:** Protect capital; build shopping list; accumulate quality companies with strong balance sheets in the final leg of the downturn.

## 2. India-Specific Cycle Overlays

### The RBI Rate Cycle
India's equity market is highly sensitive to the RBI repo rate.

| Rate Environment | Impact |
|---|---|
| Cutting cycle | Positive for NBFCs, real estate, autos; banking margins compress initially |
| Stable (neutral) | Best environment for broad-based equity returns |
| Hiking cycle | Negative for leveraged companies; positive for net-interest-income initially |

**Key insight:** The market usually anticipates rate cuts 6–9 months before they occur. Watch the 10-year G-Sec yield as a leading indicator.

---

### The Monsoon Cycle
India's ~45% rural population makes the monsoon a material macro variable.

- Good monsoon (≥95% of LPA) → Rural income up → Two-wheelers, FMCG, tractors, MFI lending benefit
- Deficient monsoon (< 90% of LPA) → Rural stress → Microfinance, rural NBFCs, agrochemicals at risk
- Uneven distribution (spatial / temporal) matters as much as aggregate rainfall

---

### The Government Capex Cycle
India's public infrastructure spend is a powerful economic multiplier.

- Pre-election years typically see acceleration in road/rail/defence orders
- Post-election consolidation is common in the first year of a new government
- Watch: Monthly NHAI toll collections, railway freight loading, power sector PLF trends

**Sectors most exposed to government capex:** L&T, defence OEMs, power equipment, cement, steel.

---

### The Credit Cycle in India
India's credit cycle is particularly pronounced due to the NBFC ecosystem.

**Boom phase:** Easy liquidity → NBFCs grow aggressively → MFIs, LAP, unsecured personal loans boom  
**Bust phase:** IL&FS/DHFL-type events → liquidity crunch → asset quality worsens → capital markets shut for weak issuers

**Warning signs of credit cycle peak:**
- AUM growth of NBFCs > 25% YoY
- Loan-to-value ratios rising in mortgage portfolios
- Unsecured personal loan share rising rapidly
- Spread between AAA CP and repo rate narrowing to <50 bps

## 4. Leading vs. Lagging Indicators for India

**Leading indicators (watch these 6–12 months ahead):**
- PMI Manufacturing (>50 = expansion)
- Credit growth YoY (RBI weekly data)
- Automobile wholesale dispatches
- IIP (Index of Industrial Production)
- GST collections (proxy for economic activity)
- Freight loading (railways)

**Lagging indicators (confirm the trend):**
- GDP print (published with long delay)
- Unemployment rate
- NPA levels in banking system

---

## 5. Common Cycle Mistakes

1. **Buying cyclicals at peak earnings** — EPS looks cheap but is at peak; the PE is actually high on normalised earnings
2. **Selling defensives in early recovery** — Staples / pharma look expensive but serve as ballast
3. **Ignoring the RBI** — India's equity market cannot sustainably re-rate during an aggressive tightening cycle
4. **Confusing sector tailwind with company quality** — Rising tide lifts all boats; falling tide reveals who was swimming naked
5. **Anchoring to the last cycle** — Each cycle has a different villain (infrastructure NPA in 2013, NBFC in 2018, COVID in 2020)
