# Stock Screening Rules

Screening filters stocks by quantitative criteria to create a shortlist for deeper analysis. A screen is a starting point, not an investment decision.

---

## 1. Quality Screen — Finding Compounders

Companies with durable competitive advantages and high capital efficiency.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| ROCE (3-year average) | > 15% | Business earns above cost of capital |
| ROE (3-year average) | > 15% | Efficient use of equity |
| Debt/Equity | < 1.0x | Conservative balance sheet |
| Operating Cash Flow / Net Income | > 0.8x | Earnings are real cash |
| Revenue CAGR (5-year) | > 10% | Business is growing, not shrinking |
| EPS CAGR (5-year) | > 12% | Per-share value is being created |
| Promoter holding | > 35% | Skin in the game |
| Promoter pledge | < 20% of holding | No distress signals |

### Interpretation:
Companies passing all 8 filters are rare. Use as a high-conviction shortlist for deep-dive research.

---

## 2. Value Screen — Finding Mis-priced Quality

Stocks trading below their historical valuation or peer group without a structural reason.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| P/E vs. 5-year median P/E | Current P/E < 80% of 5-year median |
| EV/EBITDA vs. sector median | Discount > 20% to sector |
| P/Book | < 2.0x (or < 1.0x for banks) |
| Dividend yield | > 2.5% |
| FCF yield | > 4% (FCF/Market Cap) |
| Debt/Equity | < 1.5x (avoid deep-value debt traps) |
| ROCE | > 10% (ensure business is not structurally broken) |

### Interpretation:
Value screens generate many false positives (value traps). Always verify **why** the stock is cheap before investing.

---

## 3. Growth at Reasonable Price (GARP) Screen

Balances growth and valuation — Peter Lynch's core philosophy.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Revenue CAGR (3-year) | > 15% |
| EPS CAGR (3-year) | > 15% |
| PEG ratio | < 1.5x |
| EBITDA margin | > 15% and stable/improving |
| Debt/Equity | < 1.0x |
| ROCE | > 15% |
| Market cap | > ₹500 crore (liquidity filter) |

### Interpretation:
GARP screens work best in mid-cycle market conditions. In expensive markets, PEG thresholds must be tightened.

---

## 4. Turnaround Screen — High Risk, High Reward

Companies recovering from a temporary setback (not a structural decline).

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Revenue declining → now growing | At least 2 quarters of positive growth after 4+ quarters of decline |
| EBITDA margin recovery | Improving for 3+ consecutive quarters |
| Debt/Equity | Falling year-over-year |
| Interest coverage ratio | > 1.5x and improving |
| Promoter buying | Insider buying in last 6 months |
| Sector tailwind | Industry headwinds are easing |

### Interpretation:
Use a strict position size (max 3–5% of portfolio). Confirm the reason for decline was cyclical/temporary, not structural.

---

## 5. Dividend Growth Screen — Income with Capital Preservation

Companies with a track record of growing dividends — a sign of earnings quality and shareholder alignment.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Dividend paid consistently | ≥ 8 of last 10 years |
| Dividend CAGR (5-year) | > 10% |
| Payout ratio | 20–60% (sustainable range) |
| Debt/Equity | < 0.5x |
| Free cash flow / Dividend paid | > 1.5x (dividend well covered by FCF) |
| ROCE | > 15% |

---

## 6. Capital Efficiency Screen — Asset-Light Champions

Companies that grow without requiring heavy reinvestment — the compounding sweet spot.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Asset turnover | > 1.5x |
| Capex / Revenue | < 5% |
| FCF / Net Income | > 85% |
| ROCE | > 25% |
| Working capital days | < 60 days |
| Revenue growth (3-year CAGR) | > 12% |

### Interpretation:
These are typically brand-led, IP-led, or platform businesses. They often command premium valuations — verify the growth runway before buying at high multiples.

---

## 7. Momentum + Quality Screen — Trend Following with Guardrails

Strong recent price performance combined with quality fundamentals — reduces the risk of buying broken businesses.

### Criteria:
| Filter | Threshold |
|---|---|
| 52-week price performance | Top quartile of universe |
| 6-month relative strength vs. Nifty | Positive |
| ROCE | > 15% |
| Debt/Equity | < 1.0x |
| EPS growth (TTM) | > 10% |
| Volume: 3-month avg. daily volume | > ₹5 crore (liquidity) |

### Interpretation:
Momentum is a real factor in Indian markets. The quality filter prevents chasing hyped, low-quality stocks.

---

## 8. Red Flag Pre-Screen (Exclusion Filters)

Before applying any positive screen, exclude stocks that fail these:

| Red Flag | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Promoter pledge | > 50% of promoter holding |
| Auditor qualification | Any adverse/qualified opinion |
| Consecutive CFO losses | Operating cash flow negative for 3+ years |
| Related party transactions | > 15% of revenue |
| Debt/Equity (banks excluded) | > 3.0x |
| EPS restatements | Any downward restatement in last 3 years |
| SEBI actions/exchange suspensions | Any in last 5 years |

---

## 9. Composite Scoring Framework

Weight and score each metric 1–5, then compute a weighted average:

| Metric | Weight |
|---|---|
| ROCE | 20% |
| Revenue CAGR | 15% |
| EPS CAGR | 15% |
| Debt/Equity | 15% |
| FCF conversion | 15% |
| Valuation (PEG or P/E vs. median) | 20% |

Score ≥ 3.5 → Shortlist for deep research  
Score ≥ 4.0 → High conviction candidate  
Score < 2.5 → Reject (return to watchlist only)

---

## 10. Screening Principles

1. **Screens generate hypotheses, not decisions** — every flagged stock needs fundamental research
2. **Single-factor screens are noisy** — combine at least 4–5 criteria
3. **Survivorship bias** — data vendors often exclude delisted/bankrupt companies; your universe is cleaner than reality
4. **Small-cap data quality is lower** — manually verify small-cap financial data before acting
5. **Re-run screens quarterly** — companies enter and exit criteria; the opportunity set changes
