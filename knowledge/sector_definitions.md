# Sector Definitions — NSE India

Each entry covers: definition, key economic drivers, important metrics, cyclicality, and typical valuation anchors.

---

## 1. Banking & Financial Services (BFSI)

**Definition:** Entities that accept deposits, lend money, underwrite risk, or manage assets. Includes PSU banks, private banks, NBFCs, insurance companies, AMCs, and exchanges.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Credit growth (tracks nominal GDP + 2–4%)
- Net Interest Margin (NIM): spread between lending and deposit rates
- RBI repo rate cycle (lower rates compress NIMs; higher rates expand, then compress as deposits reprice)
- Asset quality — NPAs are the graveyard of bank earnings

**Critical metrics:**
- Return on Assets (RoA): >1.5% = excellent for banks
- Return on Equity (RoE): >15% sustained
- Net NPA ratio: <1% (high quality), 1–3% (watch), >3% (distress)
- CASA ratio: higher = cheaper cost of funds = competitive advantage
- Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR): must exceed RBI minimums

**Cyclicality:** Moderately cyclical. NPAs spike in downturns; credit growth booms in expansions. Do not value on trailing P/E during NPA cycles — use P/Book instead.

**Valuation anchors:** P/Book (1.5–3x for quality private banks; 0.5–1x for PSU banks), RoE-based justified P/B.

## 2. Information Technology (IT Services)

**Definition:** Software services, consulting, BPO/KPO exports. Revenues predominantly USD-denominated from US/Europe clients.

**Key economic drivers:**
- US/EU corporate IT budgets (highly correlated with US GDP)
- INR/USD exchange rate (depreciation is a tailwind)
- Digital transformation spend (cloud, AI, ERP modernisation)
- Hiring and attrition cycles (wages are ~60% of revenue)

**Critical metrics:**
- Revenue growth in constant currency
- EBIT margin (target: 20–25% for large-caps)
- Utilisation rate (>85% is healthy)
- Days Sales Outstanding (DSO): <70 days
- Employee attrition rate (<15% annually is stable)

**Cyclicality:** Mildly cyclical — revenue is sticky (multi-year contracts) but discretionary spend gets deferred in recessions.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (18–28x for large-caps), DCF (use 10–12% WACC, 5–6% terminal growth).

## 3. Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG)

**Definition:** High-frequency, low-ticket consumer products: food, beverages, personal care, household care.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Rural income and monsoon (>40% of FMCG volume is rural India)
- Commodity input costs (palm oil, wheat, packaging)
- Urban consumption (premiumisation trend)
- Distribution reach and brand recall

**Critical metrics:**
- Volume growth vs. price growth (volume is more durable)
- Gross margin (>50% for branded FMCG)
- EBITDA margin (18–25% for majors)
- Market share trends in core categories
- Working capital cycle (negative WC = company funded by trade, which is excellent)

**Cyclicality:** Defensive. Demand is relatively stable through cycles. De-rating risk comes from high valuations, not earnings collapse.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (35–60x for market leaders), EV/EBITDA (25–40x). Premium justified by capital-light, high-ROCE model.

## 4. Pharmaceuticals

**Definition:** Drug discovery, generic manufacturing, contract research (CDMO/CRO), API production.

**Key economic drivers:**
- US FDA approvals and ANDA filings (for US-generic exporters)
- Domestic formulation growth (tracks healthcare spend + 2–3%)
- API prices (often China-dependent)
- Patent cliffs of innovator drugs (opportunity for generics)

**Critical metrics:**
- Revenue split: domestic vs. US generics vs. RoW vs. API
- R&D spend as % of revenue (>6% = pipeline-building)
- USFDA plant inspection status (OAI = risk, VAI/NAI = clean)
- Working capital: high for pharma due to receivables in regulated markets

**Cyclicality:** Largely defensive domestically; US generics business is price-erosive and lumpy.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (20–35x), EV/EBITDA (12–20x). Discount for US-heavy revenues due to FDA risk.

## 5. Automobiles & Auto Ancillaries

**Definition:** Two-wheelers, passenger vehicles, commercial vehicles, tractors, and their component suppliers.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Two-wheeler: rural income, interest rates, fuel price
- Passenger vehicle: urban income, EMI affordability, EV transition
- Commercial vehicle: GDP, infrastructure spend, fleet replacement
- Tractor: agriculture income, MSP, rainfall

**Critical metrics:**
- Volume growth (units sold)
- Realisation per vehicle
- EBITDA margin (8–14% for OEMs; 12–18% for ancillaries)
- Inventory at dealer level (>45 days = demand concern)
- EV transition progress (as % of total volumes)

**Cyclicality:** Highly cyclical. OEMs compress margins deeply in downturns. Prefer ancillary companies with diversified customer bases.

**Valuation anchors:** EV/EBITDA (8–12x for OEMs), P/E (12–20x). Discount for high cyclicality.

## 6. Infrastructure & Capital Goods

**Definition:** Construction (roads, buildings, metro), power transmission, industrial machinery, defence equipment.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Government capex (Budget allocation, PLI schemes)
- Order inflow growth (3x revenue coverage is healthy)
- Interest rates (high rates compress project viability)
- Infrastructure spending cycles (pre-election acceleration is common in India)

**Critical metrics:**
- Order book to revenue ratio (>2.5x = good visibility)
- Working capital days (infra companies often have high debtors from government)
- EBITDA margin (8–12% for EPC; 18–25% for product companies)
- Debt/Equity (EPC companies are inherently leveraged; <1.5x is acceptable)

**Cyclicality:** Highly cyclical. Linked to government spending cycles. Avoid at peaks of ordering cycles.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (15–25x), EV/EBITDA (8–14x). Order book visibility is key.

## 7. Energy (Oil & Gas, Renewables)

**Definition:** Upstream (E&P), midstream (pipelines, LNG), downstream (refining, retail), and renewables (solar, wind).

**Key economic drivers:**
- Global crude oil price (Brent)
- Refining margins (GRM for downstream)
- Government subsidy policy (petrol/diesel pricing)
- Renewable energy policy (MNRE targets, RPO norms)

**Critical metrics:**
- GRM (Gross Refining Margin): >$8/bbl is healthy
- RoACE for upstream companies
- Debt for renewables (high capex, must be modelled carefully)
- Subsidy burden for OMCs (political risk)

**Cyclicality:** Very high for upstream and OMCs. Renewables more stable with long-term PPAs.

**Valuation anchors:** EV/EBITDA (5–8x for refining), DCF for renewables (8–10% WACC).

---

## 8. Chemicals & Specialty Chemicals

**Definition:** Commodity chemicals, specialty chemicals, agrochemicals, dyes & intermediates, fluorochemicals.

**Key economic drivers:**
- China+1 strategy (India benefiting from supply chain shifts)
- Raw material (benzene, toluene, methanol) prices
- Export demand and global chemical cycle
- New capacity additions (track capex of peers)

**Critical metrics:**
- Revenue per kg / realisation trend
- EBITDA margin (12–22% for specialty; 8–14% for commodity)
- ROCE (>20% for quality specialty chemical companies)
- Capex cycle alignment with demand

**Cyclicality:** Moderately cyclical. Specialty chemicals more defensive than commodity chemicals.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (25–45x for specialty), EV/EBITDA (15–25x).

---

## 9. Real Estate

**Definition:** Residential development, commercial office, retail malls, warehousing, and REITs.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Home loan interest rates
- Urban employment and income growth
- Inventory overhang (months-to-sell)
- RERA compliance and consolidation post-regulation

**Critical metrics:**
- Pre-sales (bookings) velocity
- Collections (pre-sales ≠ cash — track collection efficiency)
- Net debt / pre-sales < 1x
- Inventory-to-launch ratio

**Cyclicality:** Highly cyclical with long lead times. Avoid developers with high debt and large unsold inventory.

**Valuation anchors:** NAV (Net Asset Value) at discount/premium, P/Book.

---

## 10. Consumer Durables & Electronics

**Definition:** White goods (refrigerators, ACs, washing machines), small appliances, cables & wires.

**Key economic drivers:**
- Household income growth and EMI penetration
- Summer intensity (ACs)
- Real estate cycle (cables and wires)
- Import substitution / PLI schemes

**Critical metrics:**
- Market share in key categories
- Working capital cycle (lower = better cash conversion)
- Brand spend as % of revenue
- Channel inventory levels

**Cyclicality:** Moderately cyclical. Premium brands more resilient.

**Valuation anchors:** P/E (25–40x for market leaders).
